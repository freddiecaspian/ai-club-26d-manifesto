01 — What We Believe

Nobody knows the ceiling

AI is the most significant platform shift since the internet. Not because of what it can do today - but because nobody knows the ceiling. Every previous technology shift had visible limits. Phones can't fly. Fibre can't be deployed everywhere.

With AI, we genuinely don't know how much better the models will get or what they'll be capable of in two years. That uncertainty is the point.

We believe most people - including most MBA students - are dramatically underestimating how fast this moves and how broadly it hits. The AI Club exists not to hype this up, but to help people see it clearly and act on it.

02 — The Platform Shift

This is PCs, the web, and mobile - all over again

Benedict Evans frames it well: AI is a platform shift on the scale of PCs, the web, and mobile. Each created entirely new value chains, new gatekeepers, new winners. But the first movers very rarely end up capturing the value. Value flows to the people who build real products and distribution, not to the people who demo well.

We're in what Evans calls the "absorption phase." Companies are automating existing workflows. Accenture reports $1.5bn a quarter in generative AI bookings, mostly pilots. About a third of large enterprises have deployed at least one GenAI solution. Another quarter don't plan to do anything yet.

Cloud is now "old and boring" but still only covers a third of enterprise workflows. Barcodes took years to deploy - but once done, enabled managing 5-10x more SKUs. The real disruption blends into the fabric and becomes unremarkable.

What this means for us

Don't chase the hype. Build understanding. The people who thrive through platform shifts are the ones who understand the structure of the change - not the ones who adopted fastest.

Sources: Benedict Evans - "The Case Against AI Stealing All Our Jobs", "AI Eats the World"
03 — The Inflection Point

We're living through it right now

Dario Amodei - the CEO of Anthropic - has publicly predicted that AI will eliminate 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs within one to five years. Many in the industry think he's being conservative.

Matt Shumer puts it more bluntly: AI isn't replacing one specific skill. It's a general substitute for cognitive work. It gets better at everything simultaneously. When factories automated, workers retrained as office workers. When the internet disrupted retail, workers moved into logistics. But AI doesn't leave a convenient gap to move into. Whatever you retrain for, it's improving at that too.

The domains already being hit: legal work, financial analysis, writing and content, software engineering, medical analysis, customer service. A year ago AI could barely write a few lines of code without errors. Now it writes hundreds of thousands of lines that work correctly.

Nothing done on a screen is safe in the medium term. That's not a prediction - it's a description of what's already happening.

What this means for us

We're not preparing for a future disruption. We're living through it. Every person in this cohort will graduate into a world where the rules of knowledge work have fundamentally changed. The AI Club exists to make sure they're not blindsided by it.

Sources: Matt Shumer - "Why No Knowledge Work Is Safe", Dario Amodei on powerful AI
04 — The Maturity Model

Most people are stuck at "Productive"

Gary Eimerman, Chief Learning Officer at Multiverse, developed a four-stage AI maturity model with his learning sciences team:

Stage 1: Literate

You know what an LLM is, you understand prompting, you know the vocabulary. Most corporate "AI training" stops here.

Stage 2: Productive

You use AI to do things faster. Same workflows, augmented. Case prep, summarising, drafting. This is where most people plateau - including most of this cohort.

Stage 3: Automation

You fundamentally rethink how you work. AI becomes a team member you delegate to, not an app you interact with. You stop prompting and start managing.

Stage 4: AI Native

The organisation is built from the ground up with AI as core infrastructure. Nobody is fully here yet.

The jump from Productive to Automation is where everything changes. And the barrier isn't technical - it's psychological. People fear making their role replaceable. The mental model of "a tool I use" is deeply ingrained. Shifting to "an agent I manage" requires a fundamental reconceptualisation of what work even is.

Was asked whether technical background predicts who's good at AI. His answer: no. The common traits are curiosity, grit, and ability to learn. Not CS degrees. Not engineering experience.

What unlocks the transition?

Leadership modelling

Gary personally pushes his EA every other week: "Why aren't you automating that?" When the leader signals it's expected, hiding stops.

Incentive structures

Quarterly Founder's Awards for building something impactful using AI. Performance measurement that includes AI adoption behaviours.

Visibility mechanisms

Sharing AI wins on all-hands. Slack engagement on shared automations. Making AI use as unremarkable as spell check.

The contrast with INSEAD is stark. At Multiverse, AI use is expected. At INSEAD, students hide it. That hidden-use culture is a symptom of being stuck at Productive - when AI feels like a shortcut, it feels like cheating. When it's embedded in your workflow at the Automation stage, hiding it would be like hiding that you use email.

What this means for us

The AI Club's job is to move people from Productive to Automation. Not to make them Literate - a YouTube tutorial can do that. The real value is in creating the permission structure, the exposure, and the community that helps people make the psychological jump.

Sources: Gary Eimerman, Multiverse - AI Maturity Model and Cultural Adoption
05 — The Taste Argument

"Good at AI" isn't a technical skill

INSEAD's Career Development Centre lists "AI & Digital Transformation" as a transferable skill employers want. No definition. No explanation. Just... AI. Tick.

The UK Government spent £4.1m on an AI Skills Hub to upskill 10 million workers. It turned out to be a website linking to courses that don't exist. Four million quid.

Research shows that students who outsource assignments to AI perform on par with students who skipped the assignment entirely. The task gets done. The grade gets awarded. The skill never develops.

Nitin Nohria, the former Dean of Harvard Business School, takes this further: in a world where AI can generate infinite variations of anything, the person who knows which one to choose becomes the most valuable person in the room. That skill isn't technical. It's taste.

AI has no taste. It can't tell the difference between something edgy and something offensive. Between something novel and something merely loud. That requires judgment, lived experience, a point of view.

What this means for us

The AI Club doesn't teach people to use ChatGPT. We help people develop the judgment to know when to use AI, when not to, and how to stay sharp while leveraging it. We care about the human, not the tool.

Sources: Nitin Nohria on taste, LinkedIn Post - "What Does 'Good at AI' Actually Mean"
06 — The Mission

What the AI Club exists to do

  1. Move people from Productive to Automation Not through lectures - through doing. Workshops where people build systems, not sit through demos. Pair a first-time user with someone who's automated their workflow. Make AI wins visible. Celebrate people who've built something that didn't exist before.
  2. Kill the hidden-use culture AI use at INSEAD feels like a dirty secret. That's backwards. The AI Club is the place where sharing how you use AI is normal - not brave. When people see their classmates building real things with AI and talking about it openly, the stigma dies.
  3. Bridge the gap between hype and reality We bring in people who are actually building with AI and deploying it at scale. Real practitioners. Real use cases. Real constraints. People who can answer "what actually went wrong when you tried to deploy this?"
  4. Make INSEAD students disproportionately prepared Every person in this cohort is about to enter a job market being restructured in real time. The AI Club gives them a head start - not by teaching tools, but by building the judgment, exposure, and practical experience that makes the difference.
  5. Connect both campuses AI adoption looks different in Asia and Europe. Different regulatory environments, different cultural attitudes, different industries being disrupted at different speeds. A global club should leverage that.
  6. Preserve learning while leveraging speed You can use AI and still learn - but it requires intention. The club should model what thoughtful AI use looks like - not just maximal AI use.
A fool with a tool is still a fool.

The most expensive mistake you can make with AI isn't using the wrong model - it's outsourcing the thinking that makes you valuable.

We're not an AI hype club. We're here because this technology is genuinely transformative, the disruption is already happening, and the difference between someone who navigates it well and someone who doesn't will be defined in the next two years.

This team exists to make sure INSEAD students are on the right side of that line.
