INSEAD AI Club 26D / Manifesto

Nobody knows
the ceiling.

Every previous technology shift had visible limits. Phones can't fly. Fibre can't reach everywhere. With AI, we genuinely don't know what it'll be capable of in two years. That uncertainty is the point. And most people - including most MBA students - are dramatically underestimating how fast this moves.

This is PCs, the web, and mobile.
All over again.

Every platform shift created new value chains, new gatekeepers, new winners. But the first movers almost never captured the value. It went to the people who built real products and distribution. Not the people who demoed well.

We're in the absorption phase. A third of enterprises have deployed at least one GenAI solution. Another quarter don't plan to do anything yet. Cloud is "old and boring" but still only covers a third of workflows. The real disruption blends into the fabric and becomes unremarkable.

50%
of entry-level white-collar jobs eliminated within 1-5 years. Dario Amodei's prediction. Many think he's being conservative.
$1.5bn
in generative AI bookings per quarter at Accenture alone. Mostly pilots. The real wave hasn't hit yet.
0
convenient gaps to retrain into. AI improves at everything simultaneously. Whatever you pivot to, it's getting better at that too.

Nothing done on a screen is safe in the medium term. That's not a prediction. It's a description of what's already happening. A year ago AI could barely write a few lines of code. Now it writes hundreds of thousands of lines that work.

The domains already being hit: legal work, financial analysis, writing, software engineering, medical analysis, customer service. The physical world is the only domain where intelligence hits a hard bottleneck. Everything else is being compressed.

You're probably stuck
at "Productive."

Gary Eimerman, Chief Learning Officer at Multiverse, built a four-stage AI maturity model. Most corporate training stops at stage one. Most MBA students - including most of this cohort - plateau at stage two.

Literate
You know the vocabulary. You understand prompting. A YouTube tutorial gets you here.
Productive
You use AI to do the same things faster. Case prep, summarising, drafting. This is where most people stop. This is where you probably are.
Automation
You fundamentally rethink how you work. AI becomes a team member you delegate to. You stop prompting and start managing.
AI Native
Built from the ground up with AI as infrastructure. Nobody's fully here yet.

The jump from Productive to Automation is where everything changes. The barrier isn't technical. It's psychological. People fear making their role replaceable.

Was technical background the predictor of who's good at AI? No. The common traits are curiosity, grit, and ability to learn. Not CS degrees. Not engineering experience. The people who make the jump treat AI like a new colleague - not a manual they need to read first.

At Multiverse, AI use is expected. At INSEAD, students hide it. That hidden-use culture is a symptom of being stuck at Productive. When AI feels like a shortcut, it feels like cheating. When it's embedded in your workflow, hiding it would be like hiding that you use email.

"Good at AI" isn't a technical skill.

INSEAD's Career Development Centre lists "AI & Digital Transformation" as a transferable skill. No definition. No explanation. Just... AI. Tick.

The UK Government spent £4.1m on an AI Skills Hub to upskill 10 million workers. It turned out to be a website linking to courses that don't exist. Four million quid.

Students who outsource assignments to AI perform on par with students who skipped the assignment entirely. The task gets done. The grade gets awarded. The skill never develops.

In a world where AI can generate infinite variations of anything, the person who knows which one to choose becomes the most valuable person in the room. That skill isn't technical. It's taste. - Nitin Nohria, former Dean of Harvard Business School

What this club actually does.

01
Move people from Productive to Automation.
Not through lectures. Through doing. Workshops where you build systems, not sit through demos. Pair a first-time user with someone who's automated their workflow.
02
Kill the hidden-use culture.
AI use at INSEAD feels like a dirty secret. That's backwards. This is the place where sharing how you use AI is normal. Not brave.
03
Bridge hype and reality.
Hear from people at the cutting edge of the field. Real use cases. Real constraints. People who can answer "what actually went wrong when you tried to deploy this?"
04
Make you disproportionately prepared.
Every person in this cohort enters a job market being restructured in real time. Not by teaching tools. By building the judgment that makes the difference.
05
Connect Fonty and Singapore.
AI adoption looks different in Asia and Europe. Different regulation, different industries, different speeds. A global club should leverage that.
06
Preserve learning while leveraging speed.
You can use AI and still learn. But it requires intention. We model thoughtful AI use - not maximal AI use.

A fool with a tool
is still a fool.

The most expensive mistake you can make with AI isn't using the wrong model. It's outsourcing the thinking that makes you valuable.

We're not an AI hype club. We're here because this technology is genuinely transformative, the disruption is already happening, and the difference between someone who navigates it well and someone who doesn't will be defined in the next two years.

This team exists to make sure INSEAD students are on the right side of that line.
